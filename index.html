Log-aesthetic curves and surfaces

Three-dimensional log-aesthetic curves

The planar log-aesthetic curve of slope 0 and slope -1 are known as the Nielsen's spiral and the Clothoid, respectively. Once we deform them preserving their respective similarity curvatures we obtain their three-dimensional versions. You can see in the videos below how the deformation is performed.

Log-aesthetic surfaces

The initial curve you see appearing in each video is a three-dimensional version of a log-aesthetic curve (3D-LAC). From this curve we perform a sweep given by time-dependent functions a(t) and b(t) such that at each time t, the given curve is also a 3D-LAC. This deformation along the time direction generates a surface that we name as log-aesthetic surface (LAS).

For the log-aesthetic surface with slope 0, we considered the time functions a(t) = sin(t) and b(t) = 1. Whereas for the log-aesthetic surface with slope -1, we considered the time functions a(t) = t and b(t) = sin(t).

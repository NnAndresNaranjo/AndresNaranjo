Log-aesthetic shapes

The planar log-aesthetic curve of slope \(\alpha = 0\) and slope \(\alpha = -1 \) are known as the Nielsen's spiral and the Clothoid, respectively. Once we deform them preserving their respective similarity curvatures we obtain their three-dimensional versions. You can see in the videos below how the deformation is performed.

The initial curve you see appearing in each video is a three-dimensional version of a log-aesthetic curve (3D-LAC). From this curve we perform a sweep given by time-dependent functions \(a(t)\) and \(b(t)\) such that at each time \(t\), the given curve is also a 3D-LAC. This deformation along the time direction generates a surface that we name as log-aesthetic surface (LAS).

For the log-aesthetic surface of slope \(\alpha = 2\), we considered the time functions \(a(t) = sin(t)\) and \(b(t) = 1\). Whereas for the log-aesthetic surface of slope \(\alpha=-1\), we considered the time functions \(a(t) = t\) and \(b(t) = sin(t)\).
